\section{Objectives}
This work investigates the effects of controlled \gls{gps} manipulation on an autonomous \gls{uav} using ArduPilot SITL.
The objective is not to modify the flight controller or the mission itself, but to study whether a \gls{uav} following a predefined waypoint mission can be progressively displaced by manipulating only its simulated \gls{gps} information.

The experimental study focuses on two increasingly complex scenarios: a constant \gls{gps} offset and an adaptive/progressive position-manipulation strategy.
The latter is inspired by the concept of safe-hijacking introduced by Noh et al. \cite{noh2019tractorbeam}, but is implemented in a simplified \gls{sitl} environment.

The main objectives of the work are therefore:
\begin{itemize}
    \item to understand the interaction between \gls{gps} measurements and \gls{uav} navigation;
    \item to analyze the \gls{gps} glitch detection mechanisms implemented by ArduCopter;
    \item to determine the relationship between the magnitude and evolution of a \gls{gps} offset and the resulting \gls{uav} behavior;
    \item to investigate whether a progressive manipulation can influence the trajectory while remaining within the limits imposed by the navigation system;
    \item to quantitatively evaluate the resulting deviation from the original mission.
\end{itemize}
